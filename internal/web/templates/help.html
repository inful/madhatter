Getting Started

The dashboard is your control center. It shows your status, next HAT day, next WFH day, and upcoming leave.

If your assignment was swapped, you will see the swap icon on your HAT tag and can hover for details.

Today: On-site or WFH?

The dashboard's Today card shows the badge that describes your day:

  • On-site — you're in the office.
  • WFH — you're working from home. Either a recurring weekday, permanent-WFH, or an approved ad-hoc request.
  • On leave — you reported leave for today.
  • @conference — you're away for a conference (a leave variant).

Weekends and holidays

On a weekend or configured holiday the card header swaps the Today label for Weekend or Holiday: <name>, the status badge becomes a muted Off tag, and the WFH override buttons are hidden (there's no row to withdraw on a non-business day). The Schedule card's HAT banner renders a No support today header with the next business day underneath, so the gap between HAT banners on adjacent days isn't a silent fall-back to "Schedule". A Next business day: Mon, Sep 7 line under the status card explains why the override buttons are absent. The schedule matrix itself still skips weekends and holidays, so the day grid starts on the next business day.

I'm actually coming in today

If your projection is WFH (recurring weekday, permanent-WFH, or an approved ad-hoc request) and you're actually in the office, the Coming in instead button on the Today card withdraws today's row for you. The badge flips to On-site that day and your quota slot is freed. The contractual pattern is unchanged: the next occurrence of the same weekday is still projected as WFH unless you withdraw that row too.

Permanent-WFH members (every weekday set as recurring WFH) have a row for every business day in the current period; withdrawing today's row is the way to signal an in-office day. The button is hidden when there's nothing to override, so a stale bookmarked URL produces a typed-error flash rather than a silent success.

If you know today that you'll be in the office on a future WFH day (e.g. "I'm in next Wednesday"), use the I'll be in on [date] picker below the today button. It only lists dates you actually have a row for, so submitting the form always withdraws the right row. The contractual pattern is unchanged: a recurring weekday you withdraw early still materialises the following week. System-assigned rows and swap targets don't appear in the picker; those need a swap, not a self-override.

WFH today (unforeseen)

If you need to report WFH for today due to unforeseen circumstances (e.g. you're sick and working from home), open the Quick Actions menu (top-right of the user card) and choose WFH today. The request is created and settled inline so the dashboard reflects the outcome within the same request — no waiting for the next settlement tick. The entry only renders when you're currently On-site, today is a business day, and the WFH feature is enabled. Capacity-floor enforcement is identical to the regular request path: if today's at-work count has already hit the floor, the report is denied rather than auto-approving through. A flash banner reports the outcome (approved / denied / error). The same path is exposed as POST /api/v1/wfh/report-today and the CLI subcommand wfh report <member-id>.

WFH: requesting and withdrawing

Use WFH to submit work-from-home requests. Requests are settled by policy and may be approved or denied based on capacity rules.

Quota

Each member has a WFH quota per period: a cap on how many work-from-home days count against you. Both ad-hoc requests and contracted recurring weekdays are counted together. When your quota is full, the form blocks new requests until you withdraw an approved one to free a slot.

The Request WFH form (/wfh/request) shows your quota for the period containing the date in the date picker — not always today's period. A user who has used 2 of 2 days this month still sees "2 remaining" when picking a date next month, because the quota resets at the period boundary. The submit button disables itself when the selected period is at the limit, with a help line under the date input explaining why; holidays disable it the same way with a "this is a public holiday" notice. Pick a date in a later period — or ask an admin to mark the day — to bypass the quota.

Why a request can be denied

A request is denied only when approving it would drop the day's on-site headcount below the minimum. When that happens the request row carries a human-readable explanation in the Denial reason column. The reason is rendered under the Denied tag on your WFH list page, on the admin manage page, and in the email notification you receive — so a denied request always tells you why, never just "Denied". The most common wording is "On-site coverage would drop below the minimum (N on-site required). M members are already unavailable; approving more would leave the team under the floor." The numbers reflect the day's actual state, so a re-read at a different time will reflect a different headcount. The reason is for information only; you cannot contest a denial from the WFH list — if you believe the floor is wrong, talk to an admin.

Recurring weekdays

If a member has a weekday set as recurring WFH, the system treats every upcoming occurrence of that weekday in the current period as an approved request. Those rows appear in the WFH list alongside ad-hoc requests, all marked Approved. Withdrawing a single occurrence frees one quota slot for that period; the next period reverts to the configured schedule.

If you self-withdraw a recurring occurrence and then re-request the same date, the new request is treated as ad-hoc (not recurring) and goes through normal settlement — so it may be denied if on-site coverage is at the floor. The recurring pattern for that weekday resumes from the next occurrence onward.

Withdrawing

Approved requests can be withdrawn by the requester or by an admin at any time before the WFH day itself. The day itself is still withdrawable, but once the day has passed the request is locked.

You can also cancel a pending request (before settlement) from your WFH list. In both cases, if you change your mind afterwards you can re-request the same date — the row is resurrected in place as a new pending request, audit fields are cleared, and it will go through settlement like any other pending request.

On the WFH list, the button is labelled differently per row type to match the user-facing intent:

  • Recurring-WFH rows show Coming in instead with a building icon. Clicking withdraws the recurring row and flips today's status to On-site.
  • Ad-hoc approved rows keep the generic Withdraw label. The user-facing intent is the same (cancel a request), but the label matches the operation.

Admin withdrawals are different: they represent an admin's decision and cannot be reversed by re-requesting. If an admin withdraws your WFH, ask them to reinstate it if you want it back. If you self-withdraw a row by mistake, an admin can re-create the request on your behalf via Manage WFH.

Leave

Use Leave to report absences. Admins review and manage leave records from the management page.

If a colleague calls in sick, any authenticated user can register that absence on their behalf via the Someone Called In Sick form (link in the user menu under Leave). The date is pinned to today — the form re-validates against the server's clock, so a tampered submission cannot file a future or past-dated leave. The same cover-assignment and maintenance machinery runs as the regular report flow; the row is always recorded as plain Leave (no leave-type selector, no email address in the member picker). A duplicate row for the same person on the same day is refused rather than stacked. If the absence is expected to last longer than today, the colleague or an admin can extend it via Manage Leave.

Each leave record carries a leave type tag (Leave or Conference). Plain Leave is the default and behaves exactly as before. Conference leave is the same in every respect except for how it surfaces on the dashboard: the "Today" status badge changes from On leave to @conference, and the schedule-matrix cell swaps its plane icon for a people-group icon and uses a distinct colour. The tag is purely cosmetic — it does not change scheduling, cover assignment, or quotas.

Calendar Subscriptions

Open Calendar to create subscription links for rota and meetings feeds.

You can copy feed URLs into your calendar app and keep schedules up to date automatically.

WFH days on the calendar feed. The per-member calendar subscription (/calendar/subscriptions) surfaces your own WFH days as separate all-day events alongside the HAT-day assignments. A subscriber sees an entry titled Alice - WFH on each day you have an approved (or contractual recurring) WFH row, so the calendar stays accurate without consulting the dashboard. Admin-marked WFH days carry a "(marked by admin)" note in the event description so calendar clients (which don't render color) can tell a self-requested day from a correction. Only approved WFH rows surface on the calendar — pending, denied, and withdrawn rows do not appear. The look is overridable via the SUPPORT_DAY_LINKS-style template path: an operator can customize the WFH event text and HTML via the standard template-override env vars.

HAT Day Swaps

Use Swaps to request exchanging your HAT day with another teammate.

Incoming requests can be accepted or rejected. Expired requests are automatically cleaned up and cannot be accepted.

You can cancel your own pending requests before they are accepted.

For admins

Admin override — marking a member as WFH

When a member worked from home today but never submitted a WFH request (e.g. they called in sick and forgot to report), an admin can record the actual state via Manage WFH » Mark member WFH. The mark is a full override: it bypasses the per-member quota and the daily on-site capacity floor, so the admin can correct the dashboard even when the math would otherwise block the action. The row is still recorded as approved, so the quota counter, the on-site count, and the calendar feed all reflect the corrected state — the dashboard is honest. The chip on the dashboard and the schedule-matrix cell render in a distinct purple-blue color (Bulma is-link) so the team can see at a glance which days were admin-asserted rather than self-requested. The mark is today-only and can be undone from the same admin page via the Unmark button on the row. Members see a "marked by admin" tag on their own WFH list and a quota notice if the mark took them past their period quota.

Assigned WFH & seat-cap picker

When WFH_SEAT_CAP is set, the system enforces a hard seat cap on the office. On every settlement pass the picker walks the next WFH_SETTLEMENT_DAYS working days and, for any date where the on-site headcount would exceed the cap, inserts a system-allocated Assigned WFH row for the excess members. Picks are deterministic — the priority is fewest voluntary WFHs in the period, then a co-presence tiebreaker (members who haven't been on-site with the cohort recently are kept on-site), then alphabetical. Assigned rows appear in the affected member's WFH list with a yellow badge; they don't burn voluntary quota. Members can swap an Assigned row with an on-site teammate via Request swap on the row. Admins can Exempt from picker on the team-edit form (a per-member checkbox) — an exempt member is never picked for involuntary WFH, but they can still volunteer via a swap. Admins can also Reassign an Assigned row from Manage WFH to move it to a different active teammate in one transaction, preserving the cap. See docs/ASSIGNED_WFH.md for the full reference.

WFH swap notifications

The WFH-swap path (Assigned / swap-origin rows only) shares the same email pipeline as HAT swaps. When you create a swap, the target receives a swap requested email; when they accept or reject, you receive the matching outcome; when you cancel a pending swap, the target receives a swap cancelled notice. The default templates read generically so the same set covers both HAT and WFH swaps. Per-member email opt-out via Manage WFH » Email preferences is honoured.

User approval

New users who sign in via an OAuth provider (Google, GitLab, etc.) start in a pending state. They see a "your account is awaiting approval" page and cannot log in until an admin approves them. Pending users appear on the team page under "Awaiting Approval" with Approve and Deny buttons.

Admins are notified by email when a new user signs in. The email links to the team page where the admin can approve or deny.

Approved users become active. An admin can also deactivate an active user (e.g. when they leave the team). Deactivated users cannot log in but their team-member row is preserved for audit. They appear under "Deactivated Users" on the team page with a Reactivate button.

Deactivation and reactivation both work by toggling a flag on the user row. Sessions belonging to a deactivated user are rejected by ValidateSession on the next request, so changing the flag is effective immediately for any future request.

WFH past-period purge

The wfh_requests table accumulates history forever by default, so a long-running deployment would grow without bound. To keep it bounded, the settlement scheduler hard-deletes rows whose date is strictly before the start of the previous quota period after each tick. The current and previous periods are always preserved.

Admins can preview or run the same purge from the web UI at /admin/wfh/purge (admin only). GET shows the cutoff and the row count that would be deleted; POST with confirm=true commits the deletion and redirects back with a flash banner. The web preview never mutates the table.

The same operation is exposed on the CLI as wfh purge (dry-run by default; pass --apply to commit, or --before YYYY-MM-DD to override the period-derived cutoff for a one-off catch-up clean). Errors with WFH feature is disabled when WFH_ENABLED=false. WFH_PURGE_ENABLED defaults to true; set it to false to opt out of the scheduler-driven cleanup without disabling the WFH feature as a whole.

How HAT Days Are Assigned

Every business day on the rota gets exactly one HAT day — the person on call that day. Two independent rotations run side by side, so a leave on one day never reshuffles the rest of the rota.

The original-HAT rotation (R1)

Each business day is assigned to the next active member in alphabetical order, wrapping around at the end of the list. The R1 index is read from a small persisted state row that records the date of the most recent original assignment and the index that was used. So Alice, Bob, Carla, Dave, Alice, Bob, … is the pattern on a four-person team with no breaks.

Because the state persists in the database, a schedule that is generated in multiple calls — for example, a 14-day rolling window that runs on every page load, or a partial regeneration of a sub-range — picks up exactly where the previous call left off. There is no in-memory cursor to clobber, so a concurrent regeneration can't roll the rotation back.

The R1 state advances only when a new original assignment is successfully written. A failed write leaves the state untouched, so a retry picks up at the same index. Weekends and configured holidays are skipped — the index only advances on a day that becomes an assignment.

The cover rotation (R2)

When the R1 member is on leave, the system needs a cover. Covers come from a second rotation that is deliberately independent of R1: it advances on its own, not as a side effect of who's on leave, so a long leave on one person cannot freeze the cover slot on another.

The cover rotation is a small piece of persistent state — a single row in the database that remembers the last date an index was computed and the index for that date. The flow is:

  1. First call ever: the row doesn't exist, so the system seeds it at the current date with index 0 — i.e. the first member in the active list. The current date is its own anchor.
  2. Same date, later call: return the stored index directly, no advance.
  3. Later date: count the working days (weekdays that are not holidays) between the stored date and the new date, add that count to the stored index, take the result modulo the team size, persist the new state, and return it. A one-day advance shifts the index by one slot; a Monday-to-Friday advance of three working days shifts it by three.
  4. Earlier date: walk backward from the stored state without updating it. This is a safety net — the system only ever looks forward in normal operation, so this branch shouldn't fire.

Because the state stores the index for the date it was computed on (not just an opaque counter), retroactive changes to the holiday list only affect the gap between the last stored date and the current query, not the entire history of the rota.

Picking the actual cover

Whichever member sits at the R2 index becomes the primary candidate for cover. If that person is also on leave for the same day, the system advances cyclically through the member list and picks the first one who is not. So covers never collide with active leave records.

What this guarantees

  • The R1 (original) and R2 (cover) for any given date are both fully determined by the team composition and the date — there is no randomisation and no dependence on the order in which leaves were reported or the order in which the schedule was generated.
  • The same person cannot be picked for every new leave (the old failure mode this algorithm replaced).
  • Across a year of random leave, the total HAT-day count — originals plus covers — comes out close to equal for every team member, with a measured spread of 1–6 days on a 5-person team over 250 working days.
  • Existing schedule entries are preserved: a leave that gets added after the rota is already generated only triggers a cover for the affected dates, never a regeneration. A regeneration of a sub-range reuses the persisted state, so the rotation continues from where the surrounding schedule left off instead of restarting from the first member.
